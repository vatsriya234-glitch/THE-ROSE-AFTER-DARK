Chapter Nine

The Second Rose

Riley didn't sleep.

Not really.

She lay awake until the sky outside her window turned from black to a pale, lifeless grey, Elias's words repeating in her head.

“Your father wasn't just there, Riley. He was the reason I disappeared.”

She had spent eighteen years believing her father was simply a man who had left. A man her mother never wanted to talk about.

But now Riley wasn't sure what she believed anymore.

She reached for the envelope Elias had given her.

It was still on her desk.

Old. Yellowed at the edges.

Her mother's handwriting was unmistakable.

Riley turned it over.

Nothing.

She opened it again.

The letter was still there.

So was the photograph.

Her mother. Elias. Her father.

And the date written beneath them.

14 OCTOBER.

Riley stared at it.

Then she noticed something she'd missed before.

A tiny red mark in the bottom corner.

It wasn't ink.

It looked like a small rose.

Riley frowned.

She turned the photograph over.

Nothing.

She checked the envelope.

Nothing.

Then something slipped from inside.

A small, dried flower fell onto her desk.

Riley froze.

It was another rose.

Smaller than the first one.

But unmistakably red.

Tied around its stem was a thin black thread.

Attached to the thread was a piece of paper.

Riley unfolded it.

The first rose wasn't hers.

Her breathing stopped.

For a moment, she simply stared.

Then her phone buzzed.

Maya: Are you awake?

Riley typed back immediately.

Riley: Come over.

Three dots appeared.

Maya: That sounds terrifying.

Riley looked at the second rose.

Riley: It is.

Twenty minutes later, Maya stood in Riley's bedroom doorway, staring at the flower.

She didn't say anything for several seconds.

Then she pointed at the rose.

“That is officially the creepiest flower I've ever seen.”

Despite everything, Riley almost laughed.

Almost.

Maya picked up the note.

“The first rose wasn't hers.”

She looked at Riley.

“Whose?”

“I don't know.”

“Your mother's?”

“Maybe.”

Maya frowned.

“Then whose was the first rose?”

Riley looked toward the desk.

The first rose was still inside the old book.

The second one lay beside it.

Two roses. Two secrets.

And suddenly Riley remembered something.

“The book.”

Maya looked confused.

“What about it?”

“The first rose was inside that book.”

Riley grabbed the book from the shelf.

She turned it over.

The leather cover was worn almost smooth.

She ran her fingers along the spine.

Then stopped.

There was something underneath the leather.

A small raised mark.

A letter.

R.

Maya leaned closer.

“R?”

Riley swallowed.

“Maybe it's not an R.”

“What else could it be?”

Riley looked at the two roses.

Then at her mother's photograph.

Then at the letter.

And for the first time, a completely different possibility entered her mind.

Maybe the roses weren't about Elias.

Maybe they were about her mother.

Maya broke the silence.

“Riley…”

“What?”

“I found something.”

Riley looked up.

Maya was holding her phone.

An old newspaper archive was open on the screen.

At the top was a photograph.

A woman standing outside Vale House.

Young. Dark-haired. Her face was partially turned away from the camera.

But Riley knew her.

She knew that face better than anyone.

Her mother.

Below the photograph was a headline.

LOCAL WOMAN DISAPPEARS FROM PUBLIC RECORDS AFTER VALE INVESTIGATION

Riley's stomach dropped.

“That's impossible.”

Maya shook her head.

“I thought so too.”

She zoomed in.

At the bottom of the article was a name.

Not Riley Rose.

Not her mother's married name.

A different one.

A name Riley had never seen before.

Rosalie Hart.

Riley stared at the screen.

Her mother's name had never been Rosalie Hart.

At least…

Riley had never known it was.

Maya slowly lowered the phone.

“Riley?”

Riley couldn't answer.

Her eyes moved to the second rose.

Then to the photograph.

Then back to the name.

Rosalie Hart.

A name her mother had buried.

A name someone had gone to great lengths to erase.

And suddenly the message made sense.

The first rose wasn't hers.

Riley looked at Maya.

“What if Rose wasn't my mother's name?”

Maya's expression changed.

“What if it belonged to someone else?”

Neither of them spoke.

Outside, somewhere in the distance, a car stopped.

Riley walked toward the window.

A black car was parked across the street.

Exactly like the one she'd seen outside Vale House.

She stared at it.

The driver's face was impossible to see through the dark glass.

Then the car slowly pulled away.

Riley's phone buzzed.

Unknown number.

You found Rosalie.

A second message appeared.

Now ask her why she became Rose.

Riley's hands went cold.

Because there was one problem.

Her mother was dead.

And whoever was texting her seemed to know something Riley didn't.

Something her mother had never told her.
