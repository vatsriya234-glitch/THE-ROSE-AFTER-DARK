A RILEY ROSE STORY

The First Lie

Sometimes the first lie is told to protect someone.

Riley didn't sleep that night.

The photograph was still lying on her desk when the sun came up. She had turned it face down three times. Each time, she turned it back.

There was something about the woman standing beside her mother that Riley couldn't ignore.

She knew that face.

Not from college. Not from the bookstore. Not from Noah.

From home.

Riley opened the old wooden box where her mother had kept the few things she had left behind.

Birthday cards. A silver necklace. A handful of photographs. And the small notebook Riley had always been told never to touch.

Her mother's handwriting filled the first few pages. Grocery lists. Phone numbers. Little reminders.

Then Riley noticed something strange.

Several pages had been torn out.

She turned the notebook over.

A folded piece of paper was hidden between the back cover and the last remaining page.

Her hands went cold.

"If Riley ever finds the photographs, tell her I was trying to protect her."

Riley stared at the sentence.

Protect her from what?

She unfolded the paper completely.

There was another sentence underneath.

"I loved him. But loving him was the mistake that started everything."

Riley stopped breathing for a moment.

Him.

There was only one person that word could mean.

Elias Vale.

But then Riley noticed something written in the corner.

A date.

14 OCTOBER.

The same date from the photograph.

The same date Noah refused to explain.

Riley reached for her phone.

She almost called Noah.

Then she remembered the message written on the back of the photograph.

Don't trust the boy.

She put the phone down.

For the first time since finding the rose, Riley wasn't wondering whether Noah was hiding something.

She was wondering whether her mother had lied to her from the very beginning.

Riley looked again at the photograph.

Her mother was smiling in it.

A smile Riley had never seen in any photograph taken after that year.

And suddenly, Riley understood something.

Her mother hadn't simply forgotten the past.

She had erased it.

Riley picked up the photograph one last time.

On the back, beneath the date, was one final line she hadn't noticed before.

"The first lie was mine."

Riley stared at the words.

Somewhere outside, a clock struck midnight.

And for the first time, Riley wondered what had really happened on October 14.
