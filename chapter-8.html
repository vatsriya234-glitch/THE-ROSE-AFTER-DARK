A RILEY ROSE STORY

The First Rose

Some secrets don't disappear. They wait to be found.

Riley stared at Elias.

"My father was the reason you disappeared."

Elias didn't deny it.

Instead, he walked toward the fireplace and took the photograph from its frame.

He turned it over.

On the back was a date.

14 OCTOBER.

Beneath it were three names.

Her mother's.

Elias Vale.

And Riley's father.

"Your mother wanted to leave," Elias said.

"She had discovered something your father had been hiding."

Riley felt the room become smaller.

"What?"

Elias looked at her for a long moment.

"The truth about why I was brought into your family's life."

Riley waited.

"Your father knew your mother loved me."

The words hurt more than Riley expected.

Her mother had loved Elias.

The letter had been telling the truth.

But there was more.

"I didn't disappear because I wanted to leave her."

Elias looked toward the old clock.

"I disappeared because your mother asked me to."

Riley frowned.

"Why would she ask you to leave?"

"Because she was afraid."

"Of my father?"

Elias nodded.

"She believed that if I stayed, you would become part of the conflict."

Riley looked down at the letter.

Suddenly, the sentence made sense.

"I loved you enough to let you hate me instead."

Her mother hadn't been protecting a perfect story.

She had been protecting Riley from a broken one.

Riley closed her eyes.

For years, she had believed her mother simply hadn't wanted to tell her the truth.

Maybe the truth had been too dangerous to tell.

Maya stepped closer.

"Then why the rose?"

Elias looked at Riley.

"Because your mother left it for you."

Riley's eyes widened.

"She left it?"

Elias nodded.

"The book was supposed to reach you when you were old enough to understand."

Riley remembered the note.

"If you found this, he hasn't forgotten."

She had assumed "he" meant Elias.

But now she wasn't sure.

"Who hasn't forgotten?" Riley asked.

Elias didn't answer.

He simply looked at the rose Riley had brought with her.

The same dried red rose.

The first clue.

The first secret.

The beginning of everything.

Some stories begin with a beautiful lie.

Riley finally understood what her mother had meant.

The first lie wasn't that Elias had disappeared.

It wasn't that her mother had forgotten him.

It was the story Riley had grown up believing about her own family.

She had spent years thinking she knew where she came from.

She didn't.

Riley folded her mother's letter and placed it inside her jacket.

She wasn't ready to forgive the lies.

She wasn't sure she ever would be.

But she was finally ready to stop running from them.

As Riley and Maya stepped outside, she looked back at Vale House.

Elias remained standing in the doorway.

"Riley."

She turned.

Elias was holding another envelope.

This one was much older.

And written across the front were four words.

"Your mother wasn't the only Rose."

Riley stared at the envelope.

"What does that mean?"

Elias looked toward the road.

A black car was parked beneath the trees.

Its headlights were off.

Someone was sitting inside.

Watching them.

Elias lowered his voice.

"It means your mother's story wasn't the first one."

Riley looked down at the rose in her hand.

For the first time, she noticed something she had missed before.

A tiny piece of paper was tied beneath the black ribbon.

She pulled it free.

There was only one sentence.

"You were never supposed to find the first rose."

Riley looked up.

The black car was gone.

And suddenly she understood.

The story hadn't begun with her mother.

It had begun long before Riley was born.

And someone had been waiting for her to remember.
