THE FIRST ROSE

The Name

Some names are buried for a reason.

CHAPTER TWO

Riley woke up convinced that everything from the night before had been a strange coincidence.

The rose.

The note.

The initials.

E.V.

She had barely slept.

Every time she closed her eyes, she saw the same dried red rose lying between the pages of that old book.

❦

By morning, Riley had decided to put the rose away.

She placed it carefully inside the top drawer of her desk, underneath a stack of notebooks.

Out of sight.

Out of mind.

At least, that was the plan.

❦

College was supposed to make things easier.

New classes. New people. A new routine.

Riley sat near the window during her morning lecture, trying to focus on anything except the questions circling inside her head.

Then Maya Carter dropped into the seat beside her.

"You look terrible."

Riley turned slowly.

"Good morning to you too."

Maya smiled.

"I'm just saying. You look like you spent the night arguing with a ghost."

Riley almost laughed.

Almost.

❦

Halfway through the lecture, Riley opened her notebook.

She stopped.

On the last page was a sentence.

"You shouldn't have opened the book."

Riley stared at the words.

Her stomach tightened.

She hadn't written them.

She knew that.

But the handwriting looked familiar.

Too familiar.

Riley quietly pulled the folded note from her bag.

She compared the two.

The same slant. The same narrow letters. The same strange way of writing the letter "t."

Whoever had written the message in her notebook had also written the note beneath the rose.

❦

"Riley?"

Maya was looking at her.

"Are you okay?"

Riley closed the notebook.

"Yeah."

It was the easiest lie she could think of.

❦

After class, Riley went back to the bookstore.

The bell above the door rang softly as she entered.

The old man behind the counter looked up.

His expression changed when he saw the book in Riley's hands.

"Where did you get that?"

Riley placed it on the counter.

"Here."

The man looked at the cover.

Then at Riley.

"That book wasn't for sale."

Riley frowned.

"What do you mean?"

He hesitated.

"It belonged to someone."

"Who?"

The man looked away.

"A woman."

Riley's heart skipped.

"What woman?"

He studied her face for a long moment.

Then he quietly said,

"She looked a lot like you."

Riley couldn't speak.

❦

When Riley finally stepped outside, Noah Gray was standing across the street.

He looked at her.

Then at the book in her hand.

"Did you find what you were looking for?"

Riley stopped.

She had never told him she was coming here.

"How did you know I was here?"

Noah didn't answer.

His eyes moved to the rose sticking out of Riley's bag.

His expression changed.

"You need to get rid of that."

Riley held his gaze.

"Why?"

Noah was silent for a moment.

Then he said,

"Because whoever left it wants you to find something."

"What?"

Noah looked at her.

"The truth."

Before Riley could ask another question, Noah turned and walked away.

❦

That night, Riley opened the old book again.

She ran her fingers along the inside of the cover.

Something felt wrong.

She pressed against the edge.

A hidden section lifted.

Beneath it was a photograph.

Riley picked it up.

Her hands began to shake.

There was her mother.

Standing beside a man Riley had never seen before.

Elias Vale.

And beside him was someone else.

But the person's face had been scratched away.

❦

Riley turned the photograph over.

14 OCTOBER

Beneath the date was another sentence.

"Don't trust the boy."

Riley stared at the words.

Then she saw the final line.

"He already knows who you are."

Riley slowly lowered the photograph.

For the first time, she wondered if Noah Gray had ever met her by accident.

❦

Maybe the rose hadn't led Riley to the mystery.

Maybe the mystery had been waiting for her.
