A RILEY ROSE STORY

The First Rose

Some stories begin with a beautiful lie.

CHAPTER ONE

The rain had been falling since sunset.

By midnight, the streets were almost empty. The city looked different beneath the rain—quieter, colder, almost as if it were hiding something.

Riley Rose stood beneath an old streetlamp, staring at the building across the road.

It was her first week in the city.

Her first week at a new college. Her first week in a place where nobody knew her, nobody knew her family, and nobody knew the things she had spent years trying not to remember.

❦

She had promised herself that this would be a new beginning.

No looking back. No questions about her mother. No searching through old photographs. No wondering why certain names were never spoken inside their house.

Especially not one name.

Elias Vale.

Riley didn't know who Elias Vale was. Not really.

She only knew that whenever she had asked her mother about him, the answer had always been the same.

"Some people belong in the past."

Riley had never understood what that meant.

Until tonight.

❦

Earlier that evening, Riley had stopped at a small second-hand bookstore near the college.

She had bought an old book because she liked the smell of its pages.

She hadn't expected anything to be inside it.

But when she opened it later that night, something fell onto the floor.

A dried red rose.

Riley picked it up carefully.

A small black ribbon had been tied around its stem.

And underneath the ribbon was a folded piece of paper.

Her fingers hesitated before opening it.

There was only one sentence.

"If you found this, he hasn't forgotten."

Riley read it twice.

Then a third time.

Her heart began beating faster.

She didn't know who "he" was.

But somehow, she already knew the answer.

❦

Riley turned the paper over.

Nothing.

She turned the rose in her hand.

And that's when she noticed the tiny initials written beneath the ribbon.

E.V.

Riley stopped breathing for a moment.

Outside, thunder rolled across the city.

Somewhere in the distance, a clock struck midnight.

And Riley finally understood one thing.

Her mother hadn't been telling her the whole story.

❦

Some stories begin with a beautiful lie.
