A RILEY ROSE STORY

The Letter

Some letters are written to be found years later.

Riley couldn't move.

The voice from the darkness had said her name.

Elias Vale.

The man everyone said had disappeared.

The man her mother had refused to talk about.

The man whose name had followed Riley from an old book to a photograph, from a photograph to a house, and now into the present.

Maya grabbed Riley's arm.

"Riley, we should leave."

Riley didn't answer.

A figure stepped into the doorway.

The light behind him made his face difficult to see.

But Riley didn't need to see his face.

She recognized the voice.

"You have your mother's eyes."

Riley finally spoke.

"You knew my mother."

The figure was silent.

Then he stepped back into the house.

"Come inside."

Maya immediately shook her head.

"Absolutely not."

Riley almost agreed.

Then Elias said something that stopped her.

"She left something for you."

Riley stepped through the doorway.

The entrance hall looked untouched by time.

Dust covered the furniture. Old photographs lined the walls. A grandfather clock stood frozen at 11:40.

Elias walked toward a small wooden desk.

He opened the drawer and removed a sealed envelope.

Riley recognized the handwriting immediately.

Her mother's.

Her name was written across the front.

For Riley.

If you are reading this, then you have finally found the truth I spent years trying to hide.

I wanted to tell you everything.

More times than you will ever know.

But every time I tried, I remembered what happened that night.

October 14 changed everything.

Elias was never the danger everyone believed he was.

I was the one who lied.

Not because I didn't love you.

Because I loved you enough to let you hate me instead.

There are things about your family that I could never explain without destroying the life we built.

If you have found Elias, then the truth has already begun finding you.

And there is one thing you must know before you trust anyone.

Your father knew what happened that night.

I am sorry, Riley.

I hope one day you understand why I chose the lie.

— Mom

Riley read the last line twice.

Her father.

The one person she had never thought to question.

She looked up at Elias.

"What happened that night?"

Elias didn't answer immediately.

Instead, he looked toward the photograph hanging above the fireplace.

Riley's mother was standing there.

Beside her was Elias.

And beside Elias was another man.

Riley stared at the photograph.

She knew that face.

She had seen it in family photographs all her life.

Her father's face.

Riley slowly turned toward Elias.

"My father was there."

Elias finally nodded.

"Your father wasn't just there, Riley."

He looked at the letter in her hands.

"He was the reason I disappeared."
