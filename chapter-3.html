THE FIRST ROSE

The Photograph

Some memories are hidden for a reason.

CHAPTER THREE

The photograph had been sitting on Riley's desk for almost an hour.

She hadn't moved it.

She hadn't looked away from it either.

Her mother stood in the centre of the photograph, younger than Riley had ever remembered seeing her.

Beside her stood Elias Vale.

Riley knew it was him because of the name on the back.

But there was another person in the photograph.

Someone whose face had been deliberately scratched away.

❦

Riley picked up the photograph again.

She turned it toward the window.

Morning light fell across the picture.

She studied every corner.

The three people were standing outside an old building.

Behind them was a tall iron gate.

On the other side of it stood a stone building covered in climbing vines.

Riley knew that place.

Or at least she thought she did.

She had passed it several times since moving to the city.

An old house near the edge of campus.

People called it Vale House.

Riley's fingers tightened around the photograph.

Vale.

The same name.

❦

She called Maya.

"I need you to do something for me."

There was silence on the other end.

"That sounds suspicious."

"Just listen."

Riley sent her a picture of the photograph.

A few seconds passed.

Then Maya replied.

"Where did you get this?"

Riley swallowed.

"The book."

Maya called immediately.

"Riley, what exactly did you find?"

Riley told her everything.

The rose. The note. The initials. The message in her notebook. The bookstore. Noah.

She left out only one thing.

She didn't tell Maya how frightened she actually was.

❦

Maya arrived twenty minutes later.

She looked at the photograph without saying anything.

Then she pointed toward the building in the background.

"That's Vale House."

Riley nodded.

"You know it?"

"Everyone knows it."

Maya paused.

"It's been empty for years."

Riley looked at the photograph again.

"How many years?"

Maya searched on her phone.

Her expression slowly changed.

"Riley..."

"What?"

Maya turned the phone toward her.

An old newspaper article was open on the screen.

The headline mentioned the Vale family.

"Local businessman disappears after October gathering."

Riley's eyes moved to the date.

October 15.

The day after the date written on the back of the photograph.

❦

Riley felt cold.

"Who disappeared?"

Maya looked at her.

"Elias Vale."

Neither of them spoke.

Riley looked down at the photograph.

Her mother had been standing beside a man who disappeared the day after this photograph was taken.

And somehow, the photograph had ended up inside a book waiting for Riley to find it.

That couldn't be a coincidence.

❦

"There's something else," Maya said.

Riley looked up.

Maya zoomed in on the photograph.

Near the bottom corner was a small reflection in the window behind the three people.

It was barely visible.

But there was someone standing inside the building.

Watching them.

Riley stared at the reflection.

"Can you make it clearer?"

Maya tried.

The image became blurry.

But one detail remained.

The person was holding something.

A small black object.

❦

The front door opened.

Riley turned.

Noah stood there.

His eyes immediately went to the photograph.

He didn't look surprised.

That was what frightened Riley most.

"You knew."

Noah didn't answer.

Riley stood.

"You knew about Elias."

"Riley—"

"You knew about my mother."

Noah looked at Maya.

Then back at Riley.

"I knew there was a connection."

"That's not what I asked."

Silence.

Finally, Noah said,

"Yes."

❦

Riley felt anger replace the fear.

"Then why did you become friends with me?"

Noah's expression changed.

For the first time, he looked uncertain.

"It wasn't supposed to happen like this."

"What wasn't?"

He looked at the photograph.

"You finding out."

Riley stared at him.

"Finding out what?"

Noah didn't answer.

Instead, he looked at the back of the photograph.

His face went pale.

"Where did you get this?"

Riley remembered the bookstore.

The hidden compartment.

The rose.

The note.

And the warning.

Don't trust the boy.

❦

"Why does the date matter?" Riley asked.

Noah looked away.

"Because October 14 wasn't the night Elias disappeared."

Riley frowned.

"Then what happened that night?"

Noah looked directly at her.

"Something happened to your mother."

Riley's heart stopped.

"What are you talking about?"

Noah took a step toward her.

"Your mother wasn't just there that night, Riley."

He looked at the photograph again.

"She was the reason everyone was there."

❦

Before Riley could ask another question, Maya noticed something.

"Wait."

She reached for the photograph.

Along the bottom edge, beneath the scratched-out face, was a thin line.

It looked like the photograph had been cut.

Maya carefully lifted the edge.

Something slipped out.

A second photograph.

Smaller than the first.

Older.

Riley picked it up.

Her eyes widened.

It showed the same three people.

But this time, the third person's face had not been scratched out.

❦

Riley knew that face.

She had seen it before.

In her mother's old photographs.
