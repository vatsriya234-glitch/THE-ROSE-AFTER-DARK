Chapter Ten

The Name She Buried

Riley didn't tell Noah about the messages.

Not yet.

She wasn't sure whether that was because she didn't trust him or because she was afraid of what his reaction might reveal.

Maya sat across from her at the kitchen table, the old newspaper still open between them.

The name stared back at them.

Rosalie Hart.

“We need to find out who she was,” Maya said.

Riley nodded.

“And why she stopped being her.”

Maya opened her laptop.

“I'm going to search everything I can find.”

Riley looked at the second rose sitting beside the newspaper.

“Start with October 14.”

Maya typed.

They spent the next hour going through old newspaper archives, university records and scanned photographs.

Most of the results were useless.

Then Maya stopped typing.

“Riley.”

“What?”

“Look at this.”

She turned the laptop around.

It was an old newspaper article from eighteen years earlier.

The headline was short.

WOMAN LEAVES CITY AFTER VALE INCIDENT

Beneath it was a photograph.

Riley's mother stood outside an old railway station.

She was holding a small suitcase.

And beside her was a man Riley had never seen before.

Maya zoomed in.

“Do you know him?”

Riley shook her head.

“No.”

But something about the photograph bothered her.

Her mother wasn't looking at the camera.

She was looking at the man.

Like she trusted him.

Or feared him.

Riley read the article again.

Most of the names had been removed from the online archive.

Except one.

Rosalie Hart was questioned regarding the disappearance of Elias Vale.

Riley stared at the sentence.

“She was questioned?”

Maya nodded.

“Which means your mother wasn't just connected to Elias.”

Riley finished the sentence quietly.

“She was a suspect.”

The room went silent.

Riley stood and walked toward the window.

Her mother had spent years telling her that the past was complicated.

But complicated wasn't the same as innocent.

Her phone buzzed.

Unknown number.

Riley looked at the screen.

You are looking in the wrong place.

Another message appeared.

Rosalie didn't disappear.

Riley's fingers tightened around the phone.

A third message appeared.

She changed her name.

Maya stood up.

“Riley…”

Riley didn't answer.

She was staring at the photograph again.

At the man beside her mother.

Suddenly, she noticed something.

A small silver pin on his coat.

A rose.

The same symbol that had appeared on the old book.

Riley stepped closer to the screen.

“Maya.”

“Yeah?”

“That rose.”

Maya looked at it.

Neither of them spoke.

Riley suddenly understood something.

The roses weren't simply clues.

They were a symbol.

A symbol belonging to a group of people who had known her mother long before Riley was born.

And someone had just started leaving those roses for her again.

Maya closed the laptop.

“We need Noah.”

Riley looked at her.

“Why?”

Maya hesitated.

“Because if his father knew Elias…”

She looked at the photograph one more time.

“…he might know that symbol too.”

Riley picked up the second rose.

For the first time, she wondered whether the person who had sent it wanted her to find the truth.

Or wanted her to find exactly what they wanted her to find.

And Riley was beginning to realize that those were two very different things.
